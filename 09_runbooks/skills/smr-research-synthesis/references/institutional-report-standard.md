# Institutional Secondary-Market Research Standard

## What Good Research Does

Good secondary-market research is not a summary of one report. It is an argued judgment after reading many sources, cross-checking claims, finding disagreement, and deciding what the market may be mispricing.

The report should answer:

- What is the investable conclusion?
- What exactly has to happen for the conclusion to be right?
- What does the market already believe?
- Which facts support or contradict that belief?
- Where do smart observers disagree?
- What would make us change our mind?

## Patterns From Top-Tier Public Research

- Goldman Sachs `Top of Mind` frames a contested market question, then brings in cross-firm views and weighs whether concerns are warranted or overblown.
- Morgan Stanley `BluePaper` style work uses cross-sector analyst input, ecosystem mapping, market-sizing scenarios, and a long-dated adoption path instead of a single-company narrative.
- BlackRock Investment Institute outlooks connect micro drivers to macro consequences, show charted evidence with sources, state big calls, and monitor signposts instead of treating diversification or themes as neutral assumptions.
- UBS CIO outlooks combine thesis, scenario risks, portfolio positioning, and explicit data charts instead of stopping at a directional view.

Use these as style anchors, not as sources to copy.

## Minimum Research Packet

For a high-confidence SMR stock or action report, gather:

- 3+ sell-side/external research samples from 2+ institutions.
- The latest official financial report or earnings release.
- The latest investor presentation or IR activity record.
- Management voice from earnings call, public transcript, or IR Q&A.
- Industry data relevant to the thesis: demand, capex, price, shipment, inventory, utilization, share, policy, competitor action.
- Market evidence: valuation, price trend, relative strength, flow, and liquidity.
- Bear case evidence: why the trade could fail.

If the packet cannot be gathered, show the missing pieces and downgrade confidence.

## Report Structure

1. **Executive conclusion**: one paragraph with action, confidence, horizon, and risk level.
2. **Variant perception**: what SMR believes the market underestimates or overestimates.
3. **Evidence map**: source-by-source table of claims and data.
4. **Consensus**: points confirmed by multiple independent sources.
5. **Divergence**: disagreements in forecast, margin, target price, timing, risk, or valuation.
6. **First-party check**: what company filings/calls actually say.
7. **Industry data check**: what hard industry data confirms or rejects.
8. **Valuation and scenario**: base/upside/downside with assumptions, not only target price.
9. **Technical and flow**: timing, stop, and execution support.
10. **Falsification plan**: kill triggers, add triggers, reduce triggers, and next evidence date.

## Forbidden Shortcuts

- "X brokerage says buy, therefore SMR buys."
- "A report model shows EPS up, therefore fundamentals are improving."
- "The stock broke out, therefore the thesis is confirmed."
- "Official material is fresh, therefore it supports the thesis."
- "No contradictory evidence found" when the system did not actually search for contradictory evidence.

## Confidence Language

- Use `research conclusion` only when the minimum packet is mostly complete.
- Use `research hypothesis` when evidence is plausible but incomplete.
- Use `material note` when the system is only relaying source content.
- Use `price signal` when the case is mostly technical.
